Financial rule and period accounting
For each stock, q(d)=1 when RET(d-1)>0 and zero otherwise, for close(d) to close(d+1). The prior return is lagged one full session; actual historical release times remain unverified. The first entry is December 31, 2021, so return intervals end on every analysis date from January 3, 2022 through December 31, 2024. The two warmup dates are not extra performance observations. Each sleeve begins flat and ends with fixed terminal liquidation. Ten sleeves have equal initial capital, no daily cross-stock rebalance, zero cash yield, no shorts and no leverage.

The exact v02 financial engine is reused. For h(d)=(ask(d)-bid(d))/(ask(d)+bid(d)), entry and exit flags identify position transitions. Consecutive long intervals do not pay a daily round trip. The unchanged interval factor is:

F(d) = [1+q(d)*RET(d+1)] / [1+k*h(d)*entry(d)]
       * [1-k*h(d+1)*exit(d)]

The fixed multipliers are k=0, k=1 and k=2. The latter two use recorded widths as modeled penalties, with the locked-quote qualification above. A crossed quote is never used to create a negative trading cost. No quote is swapped or replaced with an absolute spread. RET is a holding-period wealth reference, not an execution-price simulation. Fees, market impact, taxes, cash yield and latency are omitted. The 2x case is an uncalibrated stress, not an estimated confidence bound.

Wealth compounds within each sleeve and is averaged with equal initial weights. Annual and monthly results segment this single continuous path. Positions carry across December 31; no annual rebalance, extra liquidation or strategy reset occurs. A period return is its ending wealth divided by its prior-period ending wealth minus one. Annual and monthly compounded returns each reconcile to the full period.

Stock-period contributions require drifting weights. For sleeve i, period-start weight equals its prior wealth divided by total sleeve wealth. Multiply that weight by the sleeve's normalized period return. These contributions sum to the portfolio-period return. Using 10% for every annual contribution would be wrong. The full period alone starts each sleeve at 10%.

Gross-minus-proxy period return is a descriptive wealth-return difference, not a cash-fee total. In 2023 always-long's 1x return is slightly above its gross return because prior modeled costs change relative starting weights; the portfolio incurs no new passive trades that year. Full-period net wealth still falls as the cost multiplier rises. Held share is an unweighted proportion of invested stock-intervals, not dollar exposure. Percent, percentage points and basis points remain distinct.
