I recommend keeping this public short-horizon rule in research rather than trading it on the current evidence. It earns 76.35% gross in 2025, but matching starting stock allocations reduces its advantage over passive exposure from 10.60 to 1.93 percentage points. Its lower drawdown is meaningful counterevidence to my recommendation. Unequal market exposure and an unresolved transaction-cost input still prevent a reliable after-cost decision.

The decision and comparison
The question is whether a retail trader should use this short-horizon timing rule instead of passive exposure to the same semiconductor stocks. Both views have a plausible case: timing may improve the holding path or reduce losses, while passive exposure avoids frequent trades and can capture a strong industry market. This small public-rule study informs that decision; it does not test a private trading strategy or settle the literature.

The rule holds close(d) to close(d+1) if RET(d-1)>0, otherwise cash. Ten stock sleeves begin equally funded in December 2021, retain their capital and are never rebalanced across stocks within the continuous rule. The new annual passive comparator starts with the rule's actual weights at that year's start. This removes an initial-allocation difference; it does not equalize market risk or cash exposure.

The strongest evidence against the recommendation
The strongest objection is that 2025 is favorable even after matching allocations. The public rule gains 76.35% gross against 74.42%, and its within-year daily-close maximum drawdown is 18.21%, compared with 29.76% for the matched passive portfolio. Its mean beginning-of-interval invested-dollar exposure is 54.95%. These results are retained, rather than dismissing a strong year as merely an AI boom. Different exposure still prevents a risk-neutral comparison.

Attribution shows what the returns contain. MU, LRCX and KLAC account for 49.01% of positive stock contributions to the public rule's 2025 gross return. Monthly increments to the matched-comparison gap are especially positive in November (+14.63 percentage points) and April (+8.54), but negative in October (-8.22) and December (-8.18). Those increments are measured against period-start capital and sum to the annual gap. They are not simple monthly percentage returns or a causal decomposition of AI news.

The wider sample also matters. The matched-comparison gap is +5.35 pp in 2022, -43.32 pp in 2023, -2.58 pp in 2024 and +1.93 pp in 2025. Over 2022-2025 the public rule earns 71.40% gross against 125.92% for passive exposure from the same original allocation. Different cash exposure and zero cash yield prevent these gaps from establishing risk-adjusted superiority or inferiority. A favorable year does not establish persistence.

The largest limitation and next decision
The main barrier to an after-cost conclusion is explicit. ADI has a crossed closing quote on April 16, 2025 at a required cost event, corroborated in the underlying CIZ table. The frozen net calculator stops; no row or quote is repaired, swapped or dropped. Consequently this release reports gross results only. Fees, market impact, taxes, intraday delays, actual fills and cash yield are not measured. Available annual CRSP coverage ends December 2025; newer-frequency libraries deny this account access.

The stocks were selected in 2026, and the attribution design followed inspection of the historical results. This is retrospective analysis of a convenience universe, not prospective validation. My research-only position rests on unresolved costs, exposure differences and limited independent validation, not on a requirement that every year lose money. I would reconsider it after a documented quote resolution or a separate cost sensitivity specified before seeing its net result, followed by validation on a separately specified sample with fixed rules and explicit exposure and cash assumptions.
