# Retail timing and trading costs as a decision controversy

Reviewed October 26, 2026. This note motivates the decision question. It does not
claim that a panel of experts disagrees about this exact one-day positive-return rule.

[Barber and Odean, Trading Is Hazardous to Your Wealth, Journal of Finance 2000](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers/returns/Individual_Investor_Performance_Final.pdf)
uses a US discount-broker household sample and reports substantially weaker net
performance for the most active traders. The author-hosted published abstract and
introductory evidence support caution about treating frequent trading as an edge.

[Barber et al., The Cross-Section of Speculator Skill, Journal of Financial Markets 2014](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers/day%20traders/The%20Cross-Section%20of%20Speculator%20Skill.pdf)
uses Taiwanese day-trading records and documents persistent profitable skill for a
small subset after fees. The author-hosted published paper's skill/results section
supports the opposing possibility that net trading advantages can exist.

These studies are not direct replications or opposing estimates of the same object.
Different samples, markets, horizons and costs prevent importing their magnitudes
into this daily semiconductor rule. Broad average underperformance can coexist with
skill among a minority. The decision at issue is whether the evidence for a specified
retail implementation is strong enough to prefer it to passive exposure after costs.

Our strongest internal favorable evidence is the 2025 matched-allocation gross gap
and lower drawdown, while unresolved costs, retrospective selection and unequal
exposure support a research-only decision. This case study does not determine whether
all retail timing works, nor causally explain returns with AI-related news.

Instructor acceptance of this narrowed controversy and CRSP source remains open.
